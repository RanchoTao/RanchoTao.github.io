A few days ago I wrote down everything I am working on, everything I have promised someone, and everything I "want to do someday".

It came to nearly two hundred items.

## The shopping-cart problem

This is a classic cognitive trap, like online shopping: everything looks worth adding to the cart, and you only panic at checkout.

Each project made sense when it was added. An interesting research question, a website a friend needed, a competition, a course I wanted to catch up on. Each one alone was worth doing. Together they far exceeded what anyone could carry.

Worse, the longer the list, the less attention each item gets, the less likely it is to be finished, and so it stays on the list. The loop feeds itself.

## Not a time problem

For a long time, I and the people around me (including the AI models I asked) treated this as "not enough time". The natural fixes were to be more efficient, automate more, and run more things in parallel.

Writing the full list made it clear: **my bottleneck is not time, but ordering and closure.** I have no trouble starting things. What I lack is deciding which comes first, and actually closing what I have started.

Automation can execute a lot for me. It cannot decide what is worth doing.

## Three lines

After pruning, I kept three main lines:

1. **Study**: courses, exams, language tests, papers, and further study;
2. **Career**: internships, quant, Visual Deadline;
3. **Health**: training, diet, sleep.

Everything else either goes on a separate commitments list (things I have promised and must finish) or gets frozen or deleted.

One adjustment surprised me: **research is not its own line.** I used to list research alongside study and work. But for me research is really a skill. The papers it produces serve further study, so they belong under study. Its methods are used in quant and in products, so there it belongs to career. Listing it separately only made me feel I had another front that could expand without limit.

## Rules I want to try

Pruning once is easy. Keeping the list from growing back is hard. I plan to try a few rules:

- **Limit work in progress**: only a few active items per line at a time;
- **One in, one out**: to add a project, first close or freeze an old one;
- **Idea pool**: new ideas go into a separate pool and wait at least a week before I decide. Most ideas feel much less urgent after a week.

None of these rules is new. The hard part is sticking to them, so I am using Visual Deadline to help: its ranking model [pushes nearly finished tasks up](/posts/2026/11/importance-times-urgency/en/) instead of rewarding me for opening new ones.

## Finally

Dropping a project does not mean it had no value. Many of the frozen ideas are still interesting, just not now.

Admitting I cannot do everything is much lighter than pretending I can.
